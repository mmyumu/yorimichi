## Explicit adaptations

The screens are redrawn in HTML/CSS, without embedding JR photos or logos. They follow these documented JR West models, also used in the game's Tokyo hub: they do not reproduce every JR East or JR Central variant. Stations, fares, platforms, trains and timetables are the prototype's own. IC, QR, charging and reservation pickup are visible but disabled. One passenger, ordinary class; seat preference without a car seat map. The game uses a single ticket and a simplified total instead of the various real fare tickets and surcharges. The Shinkansen ticket keeps the date, train and seat preference. No real reservation or transaction, no real timetable.

## Konbini ATM

The ATM follows the Seven Bank machines found in 7-Eleven stores, which take cards issued abroad: a [photo of a Seven Bank ATM](https://encrypted-tbn0.gstatic.com/images?q=tbn:ANd9GcQov1aauhJcmdX8SJxFJKtCcn4LxEu_Sg2w9cUokUSh2A&s=10) provided by the user on 25 October 2026 (not included in the game) sets the machine: a red ATM sign with the accepted card networks, a pale grey cabinet with a red rim, an upper display over a tilted touch screen, the card slot and keypad on the right, the cash outlet below and a red base. The sequence is the usual one for foreign cards (transaction, card, PIN, amount by preset keys or 万 / 千円 entry, fee confirmation, receipt, card then cash); the ¥110 fee and the ¥100,000 limit per withdrawal are simplified. The same machine stands in every chain's store, only its plate naming the bank (Seven Bank, E-net, Lawson Bank). No real bank, card or transaction.

## Operating hours

The stations' hours follow JR East's in Tokyo: the Yamanote Line runs from about 4:26 to about 1:04 depending on the station and direction ([JRailPass, Yamanote Line](https://www.jrailpass.com/blog/yamanote-line); [Tokyo Tourism, Yamanote Line guide](https://www.tokyotourism.org/yamanote-line-guide/)), and stations pull their shutters down between the last and the first trains. The game rounds this to stations open from 4:30 to 1:00 and trains due from 4:40 to 0:45, the same at every station; the shutter times are the game's estimate, not taken from a source. The clock's speed, nightfall at 18:00 and the ride times are the game's own.

## Ticket gates

The first-person gate view follows a [photo of the JR Shinjuku west gate](https://www.tensho-office.com/en/wp-content/uploads/sites/2/2024/08/jrshinjuku-new1-1000.jpg) provided by the user on 25 October 2026: a row of green JR East gates with orange IC marks and green or red direction displays on their ends, under a yellow 西改札 West Gate band, in a bright hall with a beige and grey checked floor. A second photo of the same gates from an angle, pasted by the user on 25 October 2026 (source not given, not included in the game), sets the cabinets: charcoal bodies with green flanks and a bright green panel near the end, a black top sloping into a hood at the near end, which carries the small screen. The game draws its own simplified gate as two still pixel-art pictures (flat shapes projected and filled whole pixel by whole pixel in code, PixelMplus lettering), without the photo; the band reads the station's own 中央改札 or 新幹線のりかえ口. Slot positions follow the usual JR East layout (IC reader and ticket slot at the near end of the cabinet on the right, exit slot at the far end). As at JR East, the flaps stay open and only shut on a refused ticket; the refused ticket comes back out of the entry slot instead of the exit. On the way out, the gate keeps a ticket whose trip ends there, as Japanese exit gates do, and refuses one whose fare falls short: the difference is paid at a fare adjustment machine (のりこし精算機) by the gates, which issues a 精算券 to leave with, as on JR lines. As at real automatic gates, a ticket that came in at the same station is refused on the way out; the staffed window beside the gates (the ticket office's counters, as at JR stations where the みどりの窓口 adjoins the staffed gate) cancels the entry (入場取消) and returns it. That window letting anyone out, ticket problems or not, without charging, is the game's simplification. The entrance hall seen on the way out (glass doors, pillars, hanging exit signs, station name) is the game's own. The ticket's colour follows the issuing company's background pattern (JR East green, JR West blue, JR Central orange), simplified.

## JR fare map from Tokyo

Official source consulted on 23 October 2026: [JR East, 6 December 2024 — fare revision application](https://www.jreast.co.jp/press/2024/20241206_ho02.pdf), page 12, figure 2-1 "東京駅起点の普通旅客運賃（現改比較）".

The in-game diagram is redrawn in SVG from the 30 stations of this figure. The fares used are those on the **upper line proposed for 2026**, adult paper tickets (the IC fares in parentheses are excluded). The 16 distinct amounts are 160, 200, 210, 260, 350, 440, 530, 620, 720, 810, 910, 1040, 1230, 1600, 2090 and 2420 yen. Kamakura, absent from the figure, is added on a blue Yokosuka Line branch from Ofuna: 51.0 operating km (Tokaido 46.5 km to Ofuna, then 4.5 km), hence ¥1,040 in the same 2026 bands; the branch runs on to its Kurihama terminus, its other stops priced by operating km from Ofuna (Kita-Kamakura 2.3, Zushi 8.4, Higashi-Zushi 10.4, Taura 13.8, Yokosuka 15.9, Kinugasa 18.3, Kurihama 23.9 km). The Tokyo–Ofuna part of the Yokosuka Line, on its own tracks via Musashi-Kosugi, is merged into the Tokaido line on the board, as its fares are the same.

This is a **simplified JR reference map for the prototype**, not the full JR network nor a check of the fares actually in effect in October 2026: the source consulted is an application published in 2024. The map contains only the JR stations shown in that document. No subway, private railway or Shinkansen is added. Positions and routes are schematic and do not form a route planner.

The JR ticket is now an amount, with no assigned destination. The destination is chosen after purchase; any amount boards, and an insufficient one is settled at the destination's fare adjustment machine before its exit gates.

Fares between any two stations are computed the way JR does, from the shortest operating km along the lines, priced with the 2026 bands of the figure above (`src/fares.ts`); the result gives back exactly the figure's 30 fares from Tokyo, which the game checks at load. The km from Tokyo are those of the Wikipedia station tables, consulted in October 2026; between two neighbouring stops reached from opposite ways round (the far side of the Yamanote loop, the Saikyo and Chuo-Sobu lines across it), the real distance between them is used. Around Kyoto the km are rounded from JR West's timetables and priced with the same JR East bands, a simplification. Special fare zones (山手線内, 電車特定区間) and route rules beyond the shortest path are not modelled. Shinkansen tickets add a reserved-seat express charge by distance to the fare, rounded to give 5,810 yen from Tokyo to Kyoto or Shin-Osaka as on the Nozomi; past 140 km, the fare bands are the pre-2026 ones raised by the same 4.8 % as the 140 km band. The platform is chosen first, by its stairs in the corridor behind the gates: the lines are the real ones through each station (`src/rail.ts`), their track pairs loosely following Tokyo, Kyoto and Kamakura stations (Chuo 1·2, Keihin-Tohoku 3·4, Tokaido 9·10, Shinkansen 18·19 at Tokyo; Sagano 32·33 and Shinkansen 11·12 at Kyoto; Yokosuka 1·2 at Kamakura). Real Tokyo mixes lines across platforms (Keihin-Tohoku and Yamanote share 3·4 and 5·6; the Ueno-Tokyo Line's Utsunomiya, Takasaki and Joban trains use 7–10) and puts the Yokosuka · Sobu Rapid and Keiyo lines underground on their own 1–4; the game gives each line its own pair, Utsunomiya and Takasaki sharing 7·8, and numbers on through 11–16 to keep one numbering. On each pair, the first track's trains run towards the start of the line's list of stops and the second's towards its end, which puts Kamakura's trains to Tokyo on track 1 and Kyoto's Shinkansen to Tokyo on 11, as at the real stations. Every other station numbers its lines' pairs from 1 in the board's order, local lines first, lines of one colour sharing a pair: fictional. Any surplus is not refunded. Every station sells its own fares; there is no free return. Search by name was removed from the machine; the station staff can give a fare hint on request.

The older fictional Kamakura and Kawaguchiko excursions were removed: Kamakura is now a JR map fare, and Kawaguchiko, reached over the private Fujikyu line, left the game. Kamakura's JR station (Yokosuka Line, no Shinkansen) reuses the concourse every station shares; its exits follow the real station's sides (the east exit towards Komachi-dori and the temples, the west exit towards Onari-dori and the Enoden), and the right-hand side holds a NewDays, JR East's station convenience store chain, drawn in code with its name as plain lettering rather than its logo. Its platform number and the layout of its concourse are the game's.

## Machine row and wall map

The three photos provided by the user on 23 October 2026 served as the reference for the physical layout: a wide fare board above the machines, green ticket signs and pink **チャージ専用** signs, several neighbouring machines. The photos and their watermarks are not included in the game. The scene is rendered with three.js, with body and gaze movements kept separate. The default pixel-art view redraws the same layout as a 400 × 250 picture (the gate's flat-polygon projection) whose eye walks along the bank, with the same palette and painters. The `?hall=voxel` view builds the same layout from instanced cubes lit by three.js, its commuters carved from the front, back and profile frames of their sprites. The machines are simple volumes (façade with recesses, sign, tilted screen, payment block) whose pixel-art textures are drawn in code, with no external model or image. The wall map and signs remain crisp text, and the payment blocks double-density pixel art, inserted into the scene to stay legible. The machine screens use the [DotGothic16](https://github.com/google/fonts/tree/main/ofl/dotgothic16) pixel font (SIL Open Font License, [licence](src/fonts/OFL-DotGothic16.txt)), reduced to the game's characters by `scripts/subset-pixel-font.mjs`. The signs on the top-down maps use the [PixelMplus10](https://github.com/itouhiro/PixelMplus) bitmap font (M+ font license, [licence](src/fonts/LICENSE-PixelMplus.txt)), reduced the same way. The wall SVG map is the same drawing as the enlarged map described below.

The payment block follows a [photo of a Tokyo Station machine](https://ittekuru.com/wp-content/uploads/2022/12/08nov22-001-tokyo-station-ticket-machine-japan-railways-group-japan-rail-pass-jr-pass-seat-reservation-procedure.jpg?w=640) (card/Suica slot, banknotes, coin slot with a yellow rim, ticket outlet, change, keypad, QR reader, tray), simplified into pixel art drawn in code (1 pixel = 2 scene units) to stay legible. The same photo sets the payment block under each machine screen during a purchase, where it is playable: card/Suica slot, banknote slot (紙幣, ¥10,000/5,000/1,000), wide coin bowl with a yellow rim (硬貨, ¥500/100/50/10), ticket outlet, change outlet (おつり) and keypad. As on real JR machines, ¥1 and ¥5 coins are not accepted; the ¥2,000 note is left out of the game. The machines set into a façade and the signs above each follow a [row of JR East machines](https://encrypted-tbn0.gstatic.com/images?q=tbn:ANd9GcStFKYwahfm5nWXezHQvom_iAmjSpfgxNq7R_IdkYtEq-qsmtvNIZwc3Q&s=10): large Japanese then English (the Chinese and Korean lines are omitted), pink for charging, green for tickets, lavender for the Shinkansen.

The photographs [of the board above the machines](https://i0.wp.com/fun-tt.com/wp-content/uploads/2019/01/railroad-map.jpg?resize=768%2C492&ssl=1) and [of JR machines](https://c8.alamy.com/comp/FBAB45/japan-honshu-tokyo-train-station-automatic-ticket-machine-and-map-FBAB45.jpg), provided later as style references, inspire the visual density, the coloured lines and the fare labels. The SVG drawing is original; it reproduces neither the photographs nor their full map. English station names are removed from the map. The Noto Sans JP font is embedded for Japanese under the [SIL Open Font License](src/fonts/OFL.txt).

The Yamanote loop follows the order of the [30 stations in the JR East list](https://www.jreast.co.jp/estation/facility_result.aspx?SearchCategoryCd=7&mode=2&rosen=66%3D1%3D%E5%B1%B1%E6%89%8B%E7%B7%9A), including 高輪ゲートウェイ. The names of the small stations are shown on the board and in the enlarged view. Their fares are computed rather than transcribed. Each stop's shortest operating distance from Tokyo was taken from the station tables (営業キロ) of the Japanese Wikipedia articles on the [Yamanote](https://ja.wikipedia.org/wiki/山手線), [Chūō rapid](https://ja.wikipedia.org/wiki/中央線快速), [Chūō main](https://ja.wikipedia.org/wiki/中央本線), [Chūō–Sōbu local](https://ja.wikipedia.org/wiki/中央・総武緩行線), [Utsunomiya](https://ja.wikipedia.org/wiki/宇都宮線), [Keihin-Tōhoku](https://ja.wikipedia.org/wiki/京浜東北線), [Takasaki](https://ja.wikipedia.org/wiki/高崎線), [Akabane](https://ja.wikipedia.org/wiki/赤羽線), [Jōban](https://ja.wikipedia.org/wiki/常磐線), [Yokosuka–Sōbu rapid](https://ja.wikipedia.org/wiki/横須賀・総武快速線), [Sotobō](https://ja.wikipedia.org/wiki/外房線), [Keiyō](https://ja.wikipedia.org/wiki/京葉線) and [Tōkaidō](https://ja.wikipedia.org/wiki/東海道線_(JR東日本)) lines, consulted on 24 October 2026; the fare is then read from the JR East 幹線 table of the March 2026 revision, which merges the former 電車特定区間 and 山手線内 fares (kilometres rounded up; table as listed by [jr-group.jp](https://jr-group.jp/higashinihon-fare/)). This calculation reproduces all 30 fares of the JR East figure, so the two sources agree. It ignores the special routing rules (選択乗車 and the like), which do not apply to these trips from Tokyo. The calculation adds 1,410 and 1,790 yen to the board's fare list; the machine keeps the 16 amounts of the 30 destination stations.

The [new board photograph](https://ourlocalworld.com/wp-content/uploads/2024/10/Route-Map-e1728296991341.jpg) provided by the user serves as the composition reference: a white 16:9 board, the Yamanote loop in the centre, lines drawn at 0°, 45° and 90°, vertical stop names along horizontal lines, Tokyo marked by a red **現在地** arrow, Tokyo Bay at the bottom, a title with green squares at the top right and an explanatory column on the left (line legend and list of the fares, without the original's IC conversion table). The drawing is original, with no JR logo; the 30 destination stations carry a name/fare label, the others their name followed by their fare in smaller type (side by side for three digits, stacked for four, as in vertical Japanese typesetting). Its resolution is too low to transcribe these names. The SVG's intermediate stops were taken from the [official JR East map of Tokyo and its region](https://www.jreast.co.jp/map/pdf/map_tokyo.pdf). No fare was taken from the photo.

The map is drawn from the line model of `src/rail.ts`: each line its real route and stopping order (the Keihin-Tohoku from Omiya through Tokyo to Ofuna, the Saikyo Line for Itabashi and Jujo, the Utsunomiya and Takasaki lines through Oku to Tokyo, the Joban Line from Nippori and its local service for Kita-Kashiwa, the Yokosuka · Sobu Rapid through Tokyo, the yellow Chuo-Sobu local from Mitaka to Chiba by Akihabara, the Sotobo Line for Hon-Chiba), keeping the board's stations. Where lines share track, they are drawn side by side, each in its colour, with one box across them at the stations where they stop, following the photographs of the boards [above the machines at Tokyo](https://i0.wp.com/fun-tt.com/wp-content/uploads/2019/01/railroad-map.jpg) and [at Yurakucho](https://c8.alamy.com/comp/FBAB45/japan-honshu-tokyo-train-station-automatic-ticket-machine-and-map-FBAB45.jpg). Not drawn: the Musashino, Nambu, Yokohama and Shonan-Shinjuku lines, whose stations are not on the board.
