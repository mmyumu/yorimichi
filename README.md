# Yorimichi · 寄り道

An original prototype of a playable top-down travel journal set in Japan. Phaser 3, TypeScript and Vite. Scenery and characters are drawn in code, with no external assets. Maps are 480 × 320 pixels, scaled up without smoothing; the traveller is animated in four directions.

## Development

Node.js 22.12+ (tested with Node 24).

```sh
npm ci --cache /workspace/.npm-cache
npm run dev
```

`npm run build` checks TypeScript and then produces `dist/`. `npm run preview` serves the build. No application server, account or secret is needed.

## Controls

Tap/click the map to walk around buildings; tap/click an element (ticket machine, staff, gates, exits, return sign, camera) to walk to it and use it. On the keyboard, the arrows or **WASD** (keys read by physical position: ZQSD on AZERTY) move the character, and **E**, **Space** or **Enter** interact. On a gamepad, the left stick or D-pad walks, **A** interacts, **X** opens the inventory and **Y** the journal; in dialogs and on the departures board, the D-pad chooses, **A** confirms and **B** closes. Whatever is within reach gets a yellow frame. On a phone, landscape is recommended. **Inventory** opens your bag: the tickets you carry (you can hold several, each drawn as a JR ticket; they are kept on the device, `localStorage['yorimichi-save']`, until you board with them, along with where you stand: a reload puts you back on the same spot) and your wallet; **Journal** shows the three photo memories you can collect on excursions.

## Tokyo JR map and tickets

The game starts in the Tokyo concourse. Interacting in front of the machine wall at the back of the hall opens a **front view of the ticket machines**; the player starts in front of a local ticket machine. Using the selected machine opens its screen. It follows the blue look of the ordinary machines documented by JR West, in Japanese by default with an **English** button.

1. Choose **Purchase Ticket → Cash · IC Card**.
2. Look up at the **large wall map**, which holds 31 JR stations and their fares from Tokyo. **Look at the wall map**, above the machine (or **Y** on a gamepad), pauses the purchase and opens the board above the machines full size. Drag to look around, move sideways to read another part, wheel or up/down to step closer/back. When you look up at it, the map gets a yellow frame: a click, **E** or **A** opens it full size ("read the stations").
3. Read your station's fare, then look down and click (or tap) the machine: it lights up on hover, opens if you stand in front of it, and otherwise you walk up to it. **E** or **A** do the same with the machine in front of you, framed in yellow. Coming back to the same machine keeps the selection and the purchase step. All **16 fares on the map** can be bought, from ¥160 to ¥2,420.
4. Choosing an amount goes straight to payment (**Back** returns to the amounts). Pay with the coins and notes of your **wallet** (see below). The screen shows the amount due, the amount inserted and what is still to pay.
5. Take the printed ticket from the きっぷ outlet (the screen only thanks you and shows the change), for example **TOKYO → ¥260**, and your change from the おつり outlet. The ticket has no preselected destination.
6. Go through the local gates on the left (see **Ticket gates** below), then choose the station to get off at. If the fare is too low, the trip is refused and you keep the ticket. A sufficient ticket is accepted; any overpayment is not refunded.

## Ticket gates

Using the 在来線 or 新幹線 gates on the map opens a still **pixel-art close-up of one gate**, seen from where you stand, after the JR East gates at Shinjuku's west gate: charcoal and green cabinets whose black top slopes down at the near end into a hood with a small screen, an IC reader and a yellow きっぷ slot on the cabinet on your right, the flaps between, the yellow 改札 band and the platform board on the wall behind. You cannot move or look around. Your **inventory** stands beside the view: your tickets in their own pocket, as many as you carry (each drawn as a JR magnetic ticket, in JR East green, JR West blue from Kyoto or JR Central orange on the Shinkansen) and the wallet. **Drag a ticket onto the slot** (or click it). With several tickets, choose one with the D-pad, the left stick or the arrows (it is framed in yellow) and put it in with **A** or **E**. The gate reads it; if it is valid, a pixel fade takes you through to a second picture, over the exit slot (出口), where the ticket comes out, **punched** (a round hole, kept on it afterwards and shown in the inventory): **click it** (or **E** / **A**) to take it back, then the departures follow. A ticket for another platform or line, or one already punched, is refused: beep beep, the flaps shut, a red cross on the little screen, and the ticket comes back out of the slot. The gate does not say why: check the ticket and the signs. Nothing takes you to the ticket machines: walking back to them is up to you. **Esc** or **B** goes back to the concourse (a refused ticket is picked up first; at the exit slot it takes the ticket and goes on).

Search by name was removed from the machine to make reading the map part of the game. The **駅員** station staff, at the みどりの窓口 ticket office on the left, beside the central gates, offer an optional **fare hint** on request. The hint neither buys nor fills in a ticket.

On arrival, the stations on the map share the same platform scene, bearing the station's name, except **Kamakura** (¥1,040, Tokaido Line then Yokosuka Line from Ofuna), which arrives in Kamakura's own JR station (see below). The **TOKYO** sign gives a free return to make exploring easier in this prototype. The station building on the right, under its yellow 出口 sign, leads out to the generic street outside the station (below). These neighbourhoods do not yet hold new photo memories.

The map is **JR only**, with no subway or private railways. It reproduces the 30 stations of an official JR East diagram, not every station in Tokyo, plus Kamakura on the Yokosuka Line. The reference fares are those proposed for 2026 in a document published in December 2024: whether they are currently in effect has not been checked. See [REFERENCES.md](REFERENCES.md) for the source and the adaptations.

## Wallet and physical payment

The traveller carries a purse of real yen pieces: ¥1, ¥5, ¥10, ¥50, ¥100 and ¥500 coins, ¥1,000, ¥5,000 and ¥10,000 notes. It is kept on the device (`localStorage['yorimichi-wallet']`) and shown at the top of the screen; a click opens it, with a temporary **ATM** that withdraws ¥10,000.

At a machine, the screen and the payment block stay visible together, laid out like a JR East machine at Tokyo Station: card/Suica slot, banknote slot and coin bowl under the screen, then the ticket outlet, the change outlet and a keypad. The wallet stands beside the machine. Drag a piece onto a slot, or click it (or **A** on a gamepad) to put it in its own slot. Notes do not fit the coin bowl, coins do not fit the banknote slot, and **¥1 and ¥5 coins are refused**: they drop straight into the change outlet. Change is given in as few pieces as possible. Cancelling, going back or leaving the machine returns the pieces inserted to the change outlet. Each machine keeps whatever is left in its outlets (ticket, change, refunds), even after you leave the hall or reload the page: come back to the same machine to pick it up. In the hall, a machine with something left in its outlets says so in the status line. Small synthesized sounds accompany each coin, note and refusal.

Every coin beyond eight **slows the walk** by 2.5%, down to 55%. Paying with small coins and getting a big one back lightens the purse; ¥1 and ¥5 coins will have to be spent at a konbini, in a future version.

## Shinkansen and excursions

Moving right along the row leads to the green **Shinkansen** machine: destination → date → 1 adult → train → seat preference → confirmation → payment from the wallet → ticket. The reservation keeps these choices on the ticket. IC, QR, charging, bank cards and reservation pickup are visible but disabled.

Kamakura is reached with a value ticket from the JR map, like any other station. Its JR station is a concourse like Tokyo's, without a Shinkansen: its east exit (東口, Komachi-dori) walks to the temple trail, the trail's **STATION** sign walks back, and the way home is a **Kamakura → Tokyo** ticket (¥1,040) from its machines, through its central gates. Kawaguchiko was removed.

| From | Destination | Ticket | Fictional track |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Tokyo | 31 stations on the JR map, Kamakura included | Chosen amount, no destination | 3, on the left |
| Tokyo | Kyoto | Shinkansen, one way | 19, on the right |
| Kyoto | Tokyo | Shinkansen, one way | 11, on the right |
| Kyoto | Arashiyama | Sagano excursion | 33, on the left |
| Kamakura | Tokyo | Yokosuka Line, one way | 2, on the left |

For Shinkansen and excursion tickets, the wrong platform or the wrong train does not use up the ticket. The Arashiyama excursion includes a return to Kyoto through the **STATION** sign; the Kamakura trail's sign leads back to Kamakura station. Platforms, timetables, reservations and fares for these trips remain fictional or simplified for the game; there is no real transaction.

## Finding your way and keeping memories

The hub concourses, seen from above, are laid out like the first-person ticket hall: at the back, the tiled wall with the station's name over the fare map (current station in red), the Kamakura and Nikko posters and the five machines under their lit signs; on the left, the みどりの窓口 ticket office and, beside it, the central gates to the local lines, set back under their 中央改札 sign, with the corridor to the platforms running on beyond them; on the right, the Shinkansen transfer gates and their own corridor, or, in a station without Shinkansen (Kamakura), a **NewDays** convenience store whose door people come and go through (shopping is not open yet). Tokyo, Kyoto and Kamakura share this layout and differ in their name, colour, exits, board and that right-hand side; at Kamakura the fifth machine sells local tickets instead of Shinkansen ones. Two tiled pillars out in the concourse carry the yellow 出口 signs pointing to the west and east exits, whose names are also painted on the floor; people walk behind and in front of them. The floor, the yellow tactile strip and the commuters are the same as in the hall: each outfit of the first-person view comes and goes at random: in from an exit, off a train through the gates or out of the shop, sometimes stopping a few seconds at a free ticket machine, at a ticket office counter or to look around, then out through the gates, into the shop or out by an exit; two people stand reading. People are not walls: brushing past someone slows you both for a moment and nudges you aside, like squeezing through a crowd. Mostly Japanese signage, visual announcements and an animated crowd create a sense of confusion. The side exits describe the neighbourhood and lead out of the station: Kamakura's east exit to the temple trail, every other one to a generic street outside the station, the same for every station: the station front with its entrance under a sign naming the station and the exit (東京駅 東口 / TOKYO STATION · EAST EXIT), a konbini beside it (the same shop everywhere under the sign of a 7-Eleven, FamilyMart or Lawson, each station keeping its own chain; its ATM tops up the wallet; shopping is not open yet), a plaza with vending machines, planters, an area map (current spot in red) and a bus stop, the road and its crossing, and a row of shops across it. Walking back through the doors returns to the concourse at the exit you took. Only some stations will get their own map for an exit; the shops and streets are not open yet. The subway shown on the signs is decorative and not playable. There is no time limit; the only penalty is the weight of your coins, and the only audio comes from the ticket machines.

On the Kamakura temple trail and in Arashiyama, walk up to the camera and interact to collect a memory. Memories persist in localStorage on this device. The images are placeholders; importing personal photos is not implemented.

## Phone export

`npm run build:mobile` produces `dist/yorimichi-mobile.html`, a standalone file containing the game, its scripts, its SVG map and its styles. It does not need to load any external file. Open it in landscape on a phone to read the machine comfortably.

To choose another export path after building: `node scripts/export-mobile.mjs /path/yorimichi-mobile.html`.

## In front of the machines

By default this view is **pixel art**, like the gate close-up: the machine bank seen from the concourse, about four machines at a time from the foot of the board down to the floor, with the station's name and fare board above (the Tokyo fare map stays the crisp SVG laid over the board) and the concourse's commuters standing at the machines or passing in front. **← / →** (or A/D, the D-pad, the left stick; hold to keep going) walk along the bank from machine to machine, the picture following with its perspective; the machine in front is framed in yellow. **↑** raises the view to the board (at Tokyo the wall map, framed in yellow) and **↓** brings it back to the machines. A click, **E** or **A** uses what is framed; a click on another machine walks there, and a second click uses it. Busy machines, the enlarged map and Esc / **B** work as below. Adding `?hall=3d` to the address brings back the first-person 3D scene described next, unchanged; `?hall=voxel` shows that same scene, with the same controls, built of lit and shadowed voxels: the wall tiles, machines, signs (their lettering raised voxel by voxel), gates, ticket office, posters and the commuters, carved from their sprites so they turn and step in 3D.

A first-person scene, rendered in 3D with three.js at low resolution and scaled up without smoothing to keep the pixels of the rest of the game: a piece of station around a tiled wall, a large fare board under the station's name (as on the top-down map) and five machines set into a pale façade (a large Japanese/English light sign above each, a tilted screen, a payment block with card/Suica slots, banknotes, ticket and change outlets, coin slot, keypad, QR reader and tray, metal ledge), with every texture drawn in code using the game's palette. The fare map and machine signs stay crisp; the payment blocks are double-density pixel art, shown outside the low-resolution render to stay legible. Around them, the station carries on so the scenery never seems to end: tiled pillars, a dark ceiling with fluorescent lights, a yellow tactile strip, hanging signs (きっぷうりば, 中央改札, みどりの窓口, 新幹線のりかえ), travel posters for Kamakura, Nikko (the Yōmei-mon gate of Tōshō-gū) and Arashiyama, on the left the みどりの窓口 counter beside the entry gates, the yellow 出口 exit signs wrapped around the pillars on either side, on the right the Shinkansen transfer gates set back past the pillar or, at Kamakura, the NewDays front, and passers-by who are all different (women and men, schoolchildren, elderly people, kimono, masks). They are the concourse's own commuters, seen where they are on the top-down map at that moment: someone at the next machine, people going through the gates or waiting at the ticket office, the two who stand reading. You cannot use a machine while someone is at it: wait for them to finish or take another. Nobody comes to the machine you stand at, and anyone heading there turns to another; a light haze fades out the background. You can step closer to the machines, and back only a few steps. The machine screens use a pixel skin (DotGothic16 font, hard edges, bevels): local tickets, IC charge, local tickets, IC charge, Shinkansen (local tickets again at Kamakura). The reference photos provided guide the layout; they are not included in the game. The wall map shows the same 30 stations with fares as a coloured network, with station names in Japanese only, laid out like the JR East boards above the machines: Yamanote loop in the centre, red arrow on Tokyo, legend and fare list on the left. A Japanese font is embedded so the map stays legible offline; no new fares were added.
The Yamanote forms a closed loop with its 30 stops. The map also shows the names of the intermediate stops on the lines around Tokyo, taken from the official JR East map. You read the board up close. Fares remain limited to the 30 stations with fares in the JR East reference, plus Kamakura.

- **Phone**: drag on the scenery to look around; tap a machine to walk to it and then use it, tap the map to read it. There are no longer buttons under the view. In landscape, the header and status bars shrink and the view widens on short screens, so a whole machine still fits.
- **Reading the map**: clicking the map (or E / A while looking at it) opens the SVG full size: every station shows its name and its fare from Tokyo. Drag the map and zoom with the wheel (towards the cursor) or by pinching with two fingers, down to the whole map fitting the screen; on a gamepad, the sticks pan and **LT / RT** zoom out / in; **Close**, Esc or **B** goes back to where you came from: the machine screen if you opened the map from it, otherwise in front of the machines.
- **Mouse and keyboard**: click and drag to look, left/right arrows or A/D to move sideways, up/down or W/S to step closer/back, R/F or Page Up/Down to look up/down (keys read by position: ZQSD on an AZERTY keyboard). Wheel: distance; Shift + wheel or horizontal scroll: sideways movement. E/Space uses the machine in front of you, or opens the map if you are looking at it; Esc goes back to the concourse.
- **Stepping back out**: from the farthest spot, keep stepping back (S/down, the wheel or the stick) for a moment and you turn back to the concourse, as with Esc; the same goes for walking on sideways past either end of the machine bank (A/D, left/right, the wheel or the stick). The status line warns you first.
- **Standard gamepad**: left stick horizontal to move sideways, vertical for distance; right stick to look around. A uses the machine in front of you, or opens the map if you are looking at it; B goes back. On the purchase screen, the D-pad/left stick moves focus between the machine's buttons, its outlets and the wallet, A presses the button, Y opens the wall map full size (the button shows a round **Y** badge) and B steps back like the machine's **Back** button, returning any money inserted; on the home screen, or once the ticket is issued, B leaves the machine. These controls were checked with simulated input in Chromium, not with a physical gamepad.

The two pink **チャージ専用** machines open their own screen, like the others: it asks for an IC card (Suica, PASMO) in the card slot, its **Charge** and **Check history** buttons stay off, and the slots refuse money and cards alike. The player has no IC card and these machines do not sell any, so nothing can be done there yet.

The purchase stays paused while you look at the map and resumes at the same machine. Choosing another machine restarts its own screen; a ticket already issued is kept. Cancelling or taking the ticket brings you back to the machines; **Back to the hall** returns to top-down exploration. In Kyoto, this view shows simple local signage rather than the Tokyo fare map.
